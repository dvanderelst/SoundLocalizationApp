# Sound localization experiment

## The idea

We localize sound partly thanks to the shape of our outer ears (the pinnae), which filter incoming sound differently depending on where it comes from. In this experiment you will test whether wearing **artificial ears** changes how well you can tell where a sound is coming from — does it improve your localization, make it worse, or have no effect?

You will work in pairs and take turns. One of you is the **test student** (who listens, blindfolded) and the other is the **experimenter** (who runs the app and moves the sound source). The app takes care of choosing trials, randomizing the side, adjusting the difficulty, giving feedback, and recording the results — so you can focus on running a clean experiment.

## What you need

- A phone or laptop to run the app and play the sounds.
- A blindfold for the test student.
- Tape or markers to mark seven positions on the ground.
- Your set of artificial ears.

## Step 1: Build the setup

Find a spot in the teaching rooms or the hallway, keeping some distance from other pairs so that sounds from another group don't reach your test student and interfere with their answers. Refer to the sketch below to construct the setup.

Choose a spot where the test student can sit comfortably on the ground or a chair. At about **2 m (6.5 ft)** in front of that position, place **seven markers** on the ground: one in the **center** (directly in front of the student), **three on the left**, and **three on the right**. The three markers on each side correspond to three difficulty levels:

- **Green** — easiest (furthest to the side, level 1)
- **Orange** — medium (level 2)
- **Red** — most difficult (closest to the center, level 3)

## Step 2: How the experiment works

Each trial, the sound is first played from the **center** marker, then from a **randomly chosen left or right** marker. The test student — blindfolded — says whether the sound moved to the left or the right.

The difficulty adapts automatically:

- If the answer is **correct**, the next trial gets **harder** (green → orange → red).
- If the answer is **incorrect**, the next trial gets **easier** (red → orange → green).
- It never goes below green or above red.

The app handles all of this for you: it randomizes the side, tracks the difficulty, tells you where to place the sound source, and records every trial.

## Step 3: Run the app

1. **Start screen.** Enter the test student's name, choose the condition (**Real ears** or **Artificial ears**), and set the **number of trials** (e.g. 10). Click **Start**.

2. The test student puts on the blindfold. For each trial the app walks you through three steps:

   - **Center.** Hold the device at the **center** marker and press **Play sound** once. Then click **Next**.
   - **Side.** The screen tells you the **side** (left/right) and **difficulty** for this trial. Place the device at the matching marker — *out of the test student's sight* — and press **Play sound** once. Then click **Next**.
   - **Response.** Ask the test student which side the sound moved to and record their answer. The app shows whether it was correct and moves on.

   The test student may move their head, and you can replay the sound if needed.

3. The app repeats this until all trials are done, automatically adjusting the difficulty after each answer.

4. **At the end**, the app shows your **localization score** and a results table. **Download the CSV** to keep a local copy of the data, and **commit to the online database** if your instructor asks (this uploads the run to a shared database — nothing is uploaded unless you commit).

## Run it twice: with and without artificial ears

Each test student runs the whole thing **twice**:

- once choosing **Real ears** (no artificial ears), and
- once choosing **Artificial ears**.

Then **switch roles** so the experimenter becomes the test student, and repeat. By the end, each student has one run with artificial ears and one without.

## Scoring

At the end of each run the app shows a **localization score**. It is calculated from the difficulty of each **correct** trial:

- a correct **green** (level 1) trial = **1 point**
- a correct **orange** (level 2) trial = **2 points**
- a correct **red** (level 3) trial = **3 points**

The score shown is the total of these points (out of the maximum you could have scored if every trial had been correct). The full per-trial data is also in the downloadable CSV if you want to check the calculation yourself.

Compare each student's score **with** versus **without** artificial ears — did the artificial ears help?
